Taken apart

Six pieces. Each one is useless alone. The viva “show me your file” question lands on one of these.

1. The body in the room

Plain. You sit. Your eyes are about 1.2 m off the floor. The counter is 0.84 m. You do not walk through the café. You can snap-turn 45° and slide a short distance along the counter.

Jargon. XROrigin in Device tracking mode, CameraYOffset = 1.2. Snap turn only. Continuous turn, teleport, climb and grab-move are off. A clamped slide (about ±1 m sideways) lives on the wall station, because a fixed seat could not comfortably reach both the ticket and the steam wand.

Files: CafeSceneBuilder.BuildRig, SessionControls.cs, DesktopRigDriver.cs.

2. The tools

Plain. Portafilter, tamper, milk pitcher, cup. You grab them. They have weight in the sense that they trail your hand a little, and they stop when they hit the counter. When you let go they stand upright. They do not get knocked over by a neighbour.

Jargon. XRGrabInteractable with velocity tracking: the hand sets a target, physics pushes the rigidbody toward it, so colliders block it. Depenetration is capped at 0.25 m/s and velocity changes per step are capped, which is what stopped tools flinging off the counter. ToolSettle makes a released tool kinematic and upright. The ray starts at the tool, so a cup let go under the group head cannot be parked on top of the machine. Within 9 cm of its mark it slides onto the drip-tray ring. Tool colliders use a near-frictionless physics material, so they slide off surfaces instead of dragging. The tamper adds TamperGrabTransformer. It always stays velocity-tracked, so the basket and its handle block it. Within 9 cm of the mat or basket its rotation freezes vertical and it is guided onto the basket axis. The tamp reading comes from how far the base has travelled into the open basket; the colliders themselves are never set to ignore the portafilter. PitcherPourTransformer activates only when the jug is tipped near the cup; it solves the body pose backwards from a guided spout position, placing the jug sideways to the camera's right so the cup and stream remain visible.

3. The stations

Plain. Grinder (hold to dose), tamp mat (press down, read kilograms and tilt), group head (swing the handle to lock), steam wand (heat milk to 65°C), cup (pour until full).

Jargon. ToolSocket is an XRSocketInteractor that only accepts a portafilter or a pitcher. Buttons are PressButton on XRSimpleInteractable. Dials and the lock are DragControl: they read hand position along one axis, not wrist rotation, so the simulator, a headset and a mouse all work.

4. The shift

Plain. A paper ticket lists the order and what to do now. A yellow marker floats over the next thing. You cannot brew before you lock. Finishing a step unlocks the next. “Pull another shot” starts again but keeps your grind setting, which is the whole point of dialling in.

Jargon. ShiftFlow is a step enum: Briefing, Detach, Dose, Tamp, Lock, Brew, Steam, Pour, Result. Stations call Complete. OrderTicket renders the checklist. HintMarker follows stepTargets.

5. The shot model

Plain. Finer grind, heavier dose and a firmer tamp make the shot run longer and the pressure rise. A tilted tamp lets water sneak down one side, so the shot runs fast and tastes wrong even if the numbers looked fine. The dial then says Sour, Bitter, Balanced or Channeled, and what to change.

Jargon. ExtractionModel.Predict is deterministic. Same inputs, same shot, every time. It is a teaching model with the right direction, not a fluid simulation. Say that before anyone asks.

6. Sound and words

Plain. The grinder buzzes only while it is dosing. The pump hums during the shot. Steam hisses and drops in pitch as the milk heats, and a chime plays at 65°C. A quiet café murmur sits behind you. No downloaded audio files.

Jargon. AudioKit writes PCM clips at runtime. Every source is 3D (spatialBlend = 1, logarithmic rolloff). Text is TextMesh with shader Barista/WorldText so letters depth-test and do not draw through the controllers.

Put back together: one flat white

The flowchart is Figure 1. The same path, as a list you can read while someone else drives:

  1. Read the welcome board, press START. The board hides. The ticket’s red line becomes “unlock the portafilter”.
  2. Swing the group-head handle left. The portafilter leaves the machine and stays in the hand.
  3. Seat it in the grinder fork and hold GRIND. Aim for 18 g. The grind dial starts at 6.5, deliberately a bit coarse, so the first shot is usually fast and sour.
  4. Move it to the black mat. Hold the tamper upright and press. The card shows kilograms and tilt. About 15 kg, nearly level, is the house target.
  5. Offer it back under the group head and swing the handle right until it locks. Brew does nothing until this happens.
  6. Cup on the drip tray, press BREW. The needle climbs, time and yield count, espresso appears in the cup.
  7. Pitcher on the steam rest, tip in the milk, STEAM open. Close it at the 65°C chime. Over 70°C is scalded.
  8. Tilt the pitcher over the cup. When the cup is full the dial appears: verdict, numbers, and a sentence about what to change.
  9. Pull another shot. Grind setting stays. Dose, tamp and milk start again. That second attempt is the calibration loop.

The formula, slowly

time = 27.5 × e^(−0.22 × (grind − 5)) × (1 + 0.015 × (tamp − 15)) × (dose / 18)^1.6
if tamp < 5 kg, time × 0.8
if tilt > 5°, time × 0.75   (channeled)
pressure = clamp( 9 × √(time / 27.5) , 2.5, 11 )   then × 0.8 if channeled
under 25 s → Sour  ·  over 30 s → Bitter  ·  else Balanced  ·  tilt wins and becomes Channeled
Say this

27.5 seconds is a normal double shot. Grind 5 is the reference. A higher grind number is coarser, the exponential gets smaller, the shot runs faster, pressure drops, and we call it sour. More coffee or a harder tamp slows it down. A tilted tamp shortens the shot even if you pressed hard, because water found an easy path.

If he asks for a number

Grind 6.5, dose 18 g, tamp 15 kg, tilt 0: the exp term is e^(−0.22 × 1.5) ≈ 0.72, so time ≈ 27.5 × 0.72 ≈ 20 s. That is under 25 s, so the first shot is Sour. Grind 5 with the same dose and tamp is about 27.5 s, Balanced. You do not need the calculator if you can show the direction.

Live, the gauge does not jump to the final pressure. It rises over about 3 seconds, wobbles slightly, and the stream starts after a short preinfusion. Yield is scaled to 36 g by the end of the predicted time. The verdict uses the predicted time, not a stopwatch the user can cheat.

Mechanics, at the level of the code

The sections above are what you say out loud. This is the layer underneath, with the constants that are actually in the scripts. If he says the talk felt descriptive, open this and derive one block on the board.

Step machine

ShiftFlow is a single enum, not a behaviour tree. A station may call Complete(step) only when Current equals that step, so brew cannot skip the lock and steam cannot start during the shot. Completing a step moves the yellow marker to the next transform in stepTargets and plays the chime. Restart reloads the scene. Attempt is static, so the ticket can show “shot 2”, and GrindDial keeps its setting in another static (s_Saved). Dose, tamp and milk are instance state, so they reset. That split is the calibration loop: the variable you are dialling in survives, the shot you just pulled does not.

Live shot, not just the final number

Predict runs once, when BREW is accepted. The gauge does not display that pressure immediately. Each frame does this, with T and P the predicted time and pressure:

preinfusion = min(4 s, 0.3 T)
P(t) = P · clamp(t / 3 s, 0, 1) + 0.08 · sin(13 t)
yield fraction = clamp( (t − preinfusion) / (T − preinfusion) , 0, 1 )
mass in cup = yield fraction × 36 g
stream and drip audio on only while yield fraction > 0

The sine term is display noise, about 0.08 bar, so the needle is not a dead line. The ramp over 3 seconds is the pump coming up to pressure. Liquid is withheld until preinfusion ends, which is why a fast shot still pauses before the stream. When t reaches T the pump stops, the gauge drops to zero, and RecordShot stores the predicted time. A trainee cannot stretch a sour shot into a balanced one by waiting. The verdict is a function of inputs, not of how long they held the button.

Grind dial

setting = round(lerp(1, 10, drag) × 10) / 10
knob yaw = −drag × 270°
drag is hand travel along the grinder’s local X, over 0.15 m for the full range

Apply returns immediately if a shot is running, so the model cannot be edited mid-extraction. 1 is fine, 10 is coarse. The start value 6.5 is a static default, not a random roll.

Tamp, as two different signals

The collider of the tamper does not touch the basket or the mat (Physics.IgnoreCollision). Force is computed from the pivot, which is the centre of the base:

depth = bedTop.y − tamper.y   (metres the hand has gone below the puck)
kg = min(depth × 1000, 35)
visual offset = depth × (1 − 0.15)   so the mesh sinks 15% and is drawn back 85%
tilt = angle(tamper.up, portafilter.up)
haptic amplitude = clamp(kg / 25, 0, 1) every 50 ms, controller only

The hold pose is a grab transformer, which runs before physics and overwrites the target pose. It does not copy controller rotation. It keeps the yaw from the moment of the grab, then adds a fraction of how far the wrist has tilted since then:

wrist = angle between world up and (handNow × inverse(handAtGrab) × up)
guide = 1 when the base is inside 2 cm of the basket axis, 0 outside 5 cm, and only within 12 cm above the bed
tilt allowed = min(wrist × 0.3, 12°) × (1 − 0.7 × guide)
position = hand − rotation × (0, 0.085 m, 0)   then x,z lerped toward the basket by guide

0.085 m is the handle above the base, so the hand closes on the knob, not on the disk. The guide term is the basket wall: once you are over the coffee, most of the remaining tilt is removed and the shaft is pulled onto the axis. Outside that radius the tamper can still be held crooked, which is how a Channeled shot is produced on purpose.

Grab, collision, and the 90 Hz step

Held tools use XRI velocity tracking. The interactor writes a target pose. The rigidbody is not kinematic while held, so the solver can refuse the motion. Resting tools and a locked portafilter are kinematic, which is why a neighbour cannot knock them over and why the group head can parent the portafilter without a physics fight. On release, ToolSettle waits two frames (so a socket or the group head can claim it), raycasts down, and either stands the tool on a flat surface that is not another grabbable, or sends it home.

One input path

Headset grip and mouse click are two bindings on one action, XRI Right Interaction / Select. DesktopRigDriver adds <Mouse>/leftButton to Select and Select Value, and Q and E as a 2D composite on Snap Turn, in the group KeyboardMouse. It does not run when XRSettings.isDeviceActive is true, and it does not run in the Editor if an XRDeviceSimulator is in the scene. So there are never two owners of the hand.

ray = camera.ScreenPointToRay(mouse)
hand = ray.GetPoint(reach)   reach starts at 0.55 m, scroll changes it by 0.03, clamped to 0.2…1.1 m
look: yaw and pitch from the right mouse, pitch clamped to ±70°
pour: hand pitched toward 100° while Space is held, at 160°/s

Dials do not read that wrist angle. DragControl projects hand translation onto one axis in a frame that does not rotate with the knob: value += dot(hand − handAtGrab, axis) / metresForFullRange. That is why the same dial works for a tracked controller, the simulator, and a mouse that only supplies a point.

Sound, sample by sample

AudioKit fills a float[] at 44100 Hz, mono, once per clip, then caches it. No asset files. Each AudioSource is fully spatial (spatialBlend = 1), logarithmic rolloff, min distance 0.35 m, max 12 m, doppler off. The café bed is the same clip with min distance 1.5 m, spread 120°, volume 0.25, played from behind the seat.

Text shader

Unity’s font shader does not depth-test in URP, so ticket text drew over the controller. Barista/WorldText is an unlit transparent pass: ZTest LEqual, ZWrite Off, queue Transparent. Coverage is the font alpha, hardened with smoothstep around 0.5 using fwidth so the edge stays one pixel. It does not sample neighbouring texels. An earlier outline did, and because glyphs sit against each other in the atlas the outline picked up the next letter and bloomed under the pendant lights.

Decisions he will ask you to defend

Why VR, not a phone AR overlay on the real machine?

D.I.C.E. says use immersive tech when the real task is dangerous, impossible, counterproductive or expensive. Practising on the live group head during service is counterproductive (customers wait) and expensive (about 18 g of coffee and a jug of milk per shot). AR would still need the real machine, the real beans and the real floor. Replacing the café removes all of that, and lets us show pressure and time that a real machine hides.

Why seated, device origin, snap turn?

The task is a reach in front of a seated person, not a tour of a building. Device origin places the counter relative to the headset at start, with eyes at 1.2 m, so a desk simulator and a seated headset match. Snap turn is instantaneous, so it does not create vection (the feeling that you are moving when you are not), which is the usual route to cybersickness. We later added a slow, clamped slide because a frozen seat could not reach the ticket and the wand. We did not add smooth turn, teleport or flying.

Why is the tamper a full pass-through on the basket, with no physics push back?

Say this exactly. Do not upgrade it. The basket and the tamp mat ignore collision with the tamper. There is no spring force on the rigidbody. Resistance is visual: the mesh only follows 15% of the hand’s downward travel (compliance = 0.15). Force on the card is depth × 1000 kg/m, capped at 35 kg. A headset vibrates with that force. A mouse does not. We did it this way because a real physics spring fought velocity tracking and flung the tamper. The number is what the extraction model reads, not the physics engine.

Why do other tools collide, then?

Because passing through the counter felt broken, and kinematic grabs cannot both follow the hand and stop on contact. Velocity tracking lets the solver block the cup on the counter. The fling was a separate bug: when two colliders overlapped, the solver separated them too fast. Capping that speed fixed the jump without giving up collisions.

Why generate the scene in an Editor script?

The layout changed many times (text size, board positions, collision). Rebuilding from code means the Windows player and the Editor cannot drift apart, as long as you use our build menu. Hand-editing the scene and then building would ship the old café. That happened once.

Why no downloaded models or café audio?

Licensing and credits. Primitives and synthesised clips are ours to explain. The credits board names the Unity packages under the Companion License, the built-in font, and that AI use is disclosed in the presentation. If you used anything else, add it before the demo.

Questions, with the answer in one breath

What is presence, and where is it in this project?

The feeling that the place is consistent. One café, instructions on paper, the gauge on the machine, sound coming from the object that makes it. No floating menu in a void. We did not measure presence. The simulator cannot. We designed for it (Slater and Wilbur’s coherence idea) and we should say we did not prove it.

What is cybersickness, and what did you refuse to add?

Discomfort from sensory conflict, often from vection. We refused smooth turn, artificial locomotion through the room, and flying. Snap turn and a slow slide are the only motion. We have not run a headset comfort study. Say that.

What is vergence-accommodation conflict?

In a headset the lenses fix the focus distance, but your eyes converge for the distance of the virtual object. Those two disagree, especially for small text up close. Our long reading boards are on the back wall, not 40 cm from the face. Tools are in arm’s reach, but they are not paragraphs.

Walk the first shot. Why is it sour?

Grind starts at 6.5. Coarser than the reference of 5, so time drops below 25 s. The dial says grind finer. Retry keeps the dial. That is dialling in.

What if I tamp hard but tilted?

Tilt over 5° multiplies time by 0.75 and pressure by 0.8, and the verdict is Channeled even if the shot would otherwise have been balanced. Water took the low side. The advice says keep the tamper level.

What stops me brewing early?

ShiftFlow only accepts brew on the Brew step, and ExtractionModel also checks the lock and that the cup is under the group head. The ticket tells you why.

How does the mouse grab without a headset?

DesktopRigDriver poses the right controller on a ray from the mouse, at a reach you change with the scroll wheel. It adds mouse click onto the same XRI Select action the headset grip uses. It does not run in the Editor if the Device Simulator is present.

Why 90 Hz physics?

The default fixed step is 50 Hz, so a 72–90 Hz headset moved the hand between physics ticks and the held object stuttered. Time.fixedDeltaTime = 1/90 lines them up. More solver iterations make contacts less spongy.

Show me a bug you understood, not one you pasted a fix for.

Pick the depenetration one or the old exe one. Both have a cause you can draw: overlap separation speed, and “Play is memory, the build is the file on disk”.

What is out of scope?

Latte art, real fluid, multiplayer, customers, a measured comfort study, HRTF audio (we use Unity’s built-in 3D panning). Name them before he does.

AI, in the words you should actually use

Do not say “we wrote every line”

Cursor’s agent generated most of the C# , the scene builder and the first draft of the presentation notes, in this workspace, from the brief. That is high assistance. The group’s job, and the thing he is marking, is that you ran it, rejected parts, and can explain the remainder.

A fair disclosure sentence We used Cursor’s coding agent to draft scripts and the scene builder from the brief. We then tested in Play mode, found collisions, text, layout and build problems, and changed the design: wall-mounted station controls, a flat welcome board, velocity tracking with a depenetration cap, and a tamper that stays upright. Each of us can open the file we own and explain the method that runs during the demo. We did not present generated text we cannot defend.

If a person cannot explain their row in the contribution table, do not give them that row. Split the work again before the day. Responsible use here means: you know the formula, you know what is faked (tamp spring, espresso fluid, milk), and you know what the simulator cannot prove.

Things the agent got wrong that you should mention

  • A first grab setup felt physical and then flung tools. A second setup was stable and then ignored collisions. The current one is the compromise, and you should be able to say why both earlier ones failed.
  • Text outlines and a close welcome board looked worse in the headset view than in a code review. You fixed them by looking, not by trusting the first layout.
  • An early Windows build did not contain the scene you were playing. You learned that the build reads the saved scene, so the build menu now writes that scene first.

Demo, ten minutes, in this order

  1. Start Play or the exe. Point at the seated view, the snap turn, and the station panel on the wall. Say you cannot fly.
  2. START. Unlock, dose, show the grind number. Tamp once level. Mention you could tilt, and that tilt becomes Channeled.
  3. Lock. Brew. Narrate the needle and the formula’s direction, not every constant.
  4. Steam to the chime. Pour. Read the dial out loud. Pull another shot and show the grind did not reset.
  5. Open credits from the station panel (or press K). Then, if you are in the Editor, stop and launch the exe for one mouse grab so he sees the build is the same café.
If something misbehaves

Grind does nothing: the portafilter is not in the fork, or you are not on the Dose step. Brew does nothing: not locked, or the cup is not under the spouts. Tamp card stays on “press straight down”: the tamper is not centred on the basket. No sound: the Game view speaker icon mutes the Editor only. Old café in the exe: you built with File → Build Settings instead of Barista → Build Windows Player.

“We proved it is comfortable.”We followed seated, snap-turn guidance. We did not measure sickness.
“The tamp has real resistance.”Visual compliance, a force number, and haptics on a controller. No rigidbody spring.
“The espresso is simulated.”A deterministic teaching formula, then a stream mesh and a fill amount.
“Any headset works.”OpenXR is set up. Only claim profiles you have worn.
“The slide notes match every switch.”Early notes say there is no translation. The build has a short clamped slide. Explain the change.
“Physics feels like a real cup in the hand.”Velocity tracking with caps is a compromise between collision and stability. Fast pushes still lag, and a held tool can be nudged by contacts.
